## Also a tool for the humans

- **Structures the debate**: one axis at a time
- Stance, scope, disclosure, autonomy, models, concerns
- **Manages expectations**: rules seen before effort
- One referenceable boundary, not one per PR

**"Should we allow AI?" goes in circles.<br />An agenda of smaller questions does not.**

- The same structure earns its keep before any agent reads it
- Debian's six competing General Resolution options differ almost entirely along these axes; the format is the shared frame that makes them comparable
- For contributors: fewer wasted PRs and surprise rejections; for maintainers: less reviewer burnout from "extractive" AI slop, and no re-litigating per pull request
- The `statement` pointer keeps the nuance in the prose policy, where people actually read it

**Q&A details:**

## Adoption or alignment

<!-- .slide: data-timing="0" -->

- Fedora's [Justin Wheeler](https://jwheel.org/blog/2026/09/ai-alignment/) separates two framings
- **Adoption**: "a pre-defined, non-negotiable stance"
- **Alignment**: "LLM-gen-AI use exists on a spectrum"
- Squared with a community's own values and norms

**A declaration format only makes sense under alignment:<br />where the answer is fixed, there is nothing to declare.**

[↳ README: also a tool for the humans](https://codeberg.org/open-source-ecosystem/ai-policy/src/branch/main/README.md#also-a-tool-for-the-humans)

Note:

- Both quotations are verbatim from the blog post
- Adoption's goal is simply to increase usage; alignment squares use with a community's existing values, norms and culture
- The spectrum is precisely what `stance`, `defaults` and `assertions[]` exist to carry
- "LLM-gen-AI" is the term Fedora's alignment work takes from the Software Freedom Conservancy; the documents say "AI" as shorthand for generative models, not machine learning at large
